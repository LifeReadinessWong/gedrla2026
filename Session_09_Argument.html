Petition: Protect the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge

1We, the undersigned, and A Greener America are urging members of the U.S. Congress to protect the nearly 9 million acres of unspoiled coastal plain and mountains in Alaska’s Arctic National Wildlife Refuge (ANWR). For many years, this land has been protected, but recently there has been a renewed call for accessing the oil in the Alaskan wilderness to boost the national economy and increase security.

2Today, more than 80% of Alaska’s state revenues result from the oil and gas business, a direct effect of tapping into Alaska’s rich energy reserves. Once again, economic interests in oil drilling and production of natural gas and coal are vying with environmental concerns, and the ANWR is in danger. The damage that could be done to the ecosystem would simply be too great.

3Recently, new oil and gas extraction methods have led to drilling projects beginning in other states, and the debate has resumed in Congress over whether to permit drilling in the ANWR. Because of these new drilling projects, we do not need the oil from this untouched wilderness. Therefore, Congress must prevent the destruction of the ANWR immediately to achieve the following objectives:

  • Protect the wilderness from drilling processes that cause oil spills and generate waste material
  • Continue to preserve the breeding and feeding grounds of native animal species such as caribou, snow geese, and polar bears
  • Prevent the displacement of Alaska Natives that would be affected by oil and gas drilling activities
  • Maintain the stability of local economies that rely on this land for subsistence and tourism

4We urge the U.S. Congress to prevent any future removal of natural resources in the ANWR.

Questions
Question 1
What is the main claim (thesis) of this petition?
  • AMore than 80% of Alaska’s revenues come from oil and gas.
  • BCongress should prevent drilling in the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge.
  • CThe ANWR is home to caribou, snow geese, and polar bears.
  • DNew drilling methods have opened up oil fields in other states.
Correct: B — The main claim is the central arguable position. Choices A, C, and D are supporting facts or details, not the central argument.
Question 2
Which statement from the passage is a claim rather than a fact?
  • AMore than 80% of Alaska’s state revenues result from oil and gas.
  • BThe ANWR covers nearly 9 million acres.
  • CNew extraction methods have led to drilling projects in other states.
  • DThe damage that could be done to the ecosystem would simply be too great.
Correct: D — 'Would simply be too great' is an opinion — it cannot be independently verified. The other options are verifiable facts.
Question 3
What evidence does the petition use to argue that ANWR oil is not needed?
  • AThe ANWR is home to Alaska Native communities.
  • BNew drilling methods have opened up oil fields in other states, so ANWR oil is unnecessary.
  • CThe ANWR has been protected for many years.
  • DOil spills from drilling would destroy the ecosystem.
Correct: B — Paragraph 3 specifically argues: because new projects exist elsewhere, we do not need to drill in the ANWR.
Question 4
Does this petition include a counterclaim (acknowledging the other side)?
  • ANo — it only presents arguments against drilling.
  • BYes — it admits that drilling would create jobs.
  • CYes — it acknowledges that economic interests exist and that Alaska relies heavily on oil revenue.
  • DYes — it agrees that the ANWR’s oil is needed for national security.
Correct: C — Paragraph 2 acknowledges that 80%+ of Alaska’s revenues come from oil and that economic interests exist — a concession before countering.
★ GED TEST TIP: A claim is arguable — someone could reasonably disagree. A fact is verifiable — you can look it up. Knowing the difference is essential for both reading arguments AND writing the Extended Response.
