Model Essay Body Paragraph

The following is one body paragraph from a model Extended Response essay about community solar programs. Read it carefully. Every sentence has a job. Your task is to figure out what each one is doing.

Text A provides the more specific and convincing evidence on the question of financial benefit. According to Text A, community solar subscribers typically see reductions of 10 to 15 percent on their electricity bills, with no upfront installation costs — a compelling advantage for families in Hawaii who already pay some of the highest electricity rates in the country. This specific, quantified claim directly supports the argument that community solar makes clean energy financially accessible to a broader population. However, Text B raises a legitimate concern that these savings depend on utility rate assumptions that may not hold: in markets where rates were more stable than expected, subscribers saw significantly smaller savings than advertised. This is a real limitation — but it is a design problem, not a reason to abandon the program entirely. A well-designed community solar program with transparent rate projections and consumer protections would address this concern directly, leaving Text A’s core argument intact.

This paragraph draws on the two passages from Session 17. You do not need to have Session 17 open — all the evidence you need is quoted directly in the paragraph above.

Questions
Question 1
What is the topic sentence of this body paragraph?
  • A"According to Text A, community solar subscribers typically see reductions of 10 to 15 percent on their electricity bills."
  • B"Text A provides the more specific and convincing evidence on the question of financial benefit."
  • C"However, Text B raises a legitimate concern that these savings depend on utility rate assumptions."
  • D"A well-designed community solar program with transparent rate projections would address this concern directly."
Correct: B — The topic sentence states what the paragraph will prove. Option B does this — it names the paragraph's claim before any evidence appears.
Question 2
What job does the sentence beginning with "However" do in this paragraph?
  • AIt introduces the paragraph's main claim.
  • BIt provides a direct quote from Text A to support the thesis.
  • CIt acknowledges a counterargument from Text B before the writer responds to it.
  • DIt summarizes the paragraph and transitions to the conclusion.
Correct: C — "However" signals a shift to the other side. The writer acknowledges Text B's concern before countering it — a move that makes the argument more credible, not weaker.
Question 3
Which sentence in the paragraph provides analysis (explanation of WHY the evidence matters)?
  • A"According to Text A, community solar subscribers typically see reductions of 10 to 15 percent on their electricity bills."
  • B"This specific, quantified claim directly supports the argument that community solar makes clean energy financially accessible to a broader population."
  • C"However, Text B raises a legitimate concern that these savings depend on utility rate assumptions that may not hold."
  • D"In markets where rates were more stable than expected, subscribers saw significantly smaller savings than advertised."
Correct: B — Analysis explains the significance of the evidence — it answers "so what?" Options A, C, and D are evidence or counterargument. Only B explains what the evidence means and why it matters.
Question 4
The writer says Text B's concern is "a design problem, not a reason to abandon the program." What transition word or phrase signals this shift back to the writer's position?
  • A"However"
  • B"According to"
  • C"but"
  • D"This is a real limitation — but"
Correct: D — The em dash + "but" signals the writer conceding the concern is real before pivoting back to their position. It is a more sophisticated version of "however" — same job, different phrasing.
Question 5
Label each sentence in the paragraph with its function: topic sentence, evidence, analysis, counterargument, or response to counterargument. Write each label and the first three words of that sentence.
Model answer: Sentence 1 (Topic sentence): "Text A provides..." / Sentence 2 (Evidence): "According to Text A..." / Sentence 3 (Analysis): "This specific, quantified..." / Sentence 4 (Counterargument): "However, Text B..." / Sentence 5 (Evidence of counter): "in markets where..." / Sentence 6 (Response to counter): "This is a real limitation..." / Sentence 7 (Response continued): "A well-designed community solar..."
★ GED TEST TIP: Every sentence in a body paragraph should have a job: topic sentence → evidence → analysis → (counterargument + response). If you can't name the job, the sentence might not belong.
